# Finance Fact Library: house style for UK pages

Adds to core.md. Where the two differ, this file wins for UK pages.

## Language

- British English: allowance, organise, centre, programme.
- Dates are written 6 April 2026. No Oxford comma ("A, B and C").
- Name schemes and bodies as GOV.UK does: HMRC, Universal Credit, Income
  Tax, National Insurance, State Pension, NS&I.

## Tax year

- Runs from 6 April to 5 April, written 2026/27.
- `{tax_year}` shows the year of the page's figures (from their tax_year),
  or, if none of them has one, the tax year of today's date.
- Most figures change on 6 April. Budgets (usually autumn and spring) and
  Bank of England rate decisions (eight a year) change others.

## The four nations

- Say where Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland differ, in a sentence, and
  name the nation. Common cases: Income Tax rates and bands (Scotland),
  property taxes (Scotland's LBTT, Wales's LTT), tenancy deposits, carers'
  benefits (Scotland's Carer Support Payment), student loans (Plan 4 in
  Scotland), Council Tax (England, Scotland and Wales; rates in Northern
  Ireland).
- Where all four are the same, say nothing, unless readers often assume
  otherwise (the Personal Allowance applies in Scotland, for example).

## Sources

- Point to GOV.UK (or gov.scot, gov.wales, nidirect) only where there is
  something specific to do, such as claiming.

## Rate pages

- Base Rate, NS&I, Average Savings Rates, mortgage rate pages such as
  Buy-to-Let and 2-Year Fixed, and anything else checked every 42 days or
  less. See core.md, section 2.
- Buy-to-Let: don't say it costs more than residential lending; the Bank
  of England's figures don't always show that.
